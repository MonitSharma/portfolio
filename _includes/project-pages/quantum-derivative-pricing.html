01

Where the cost of quantum pricing goes

A derivative’s price is the expected discounted payoff over market paths. Amplitude estimation estimates that average with quadratically fewer samples than classical Monte Carlo, but each sample must load a path and then evaluate the payoff coherently, as a reversible circuit. For path-dependent contracts that payoff arithmetic is a large share of the logical cost.

The published resource estimate of Chakrabarti et al. converts every simulated log return into a price with a coherent exponential. Comparing log returns directly avoids those exponentials. We start from that cheaper log-return reference and ask what else can be removed.

In these benchmarks the dates are evenly spaced, the model coefficients are fixed and the random factors sit on an evenly spaced grid. Every log return is then an affine function of a running sum of grid indices. Three ten-bit integer registers hold the whole market state of a three-asset path without wrapping.

Barrier and call tests become comparisons between integer forms of those sums and precomputed thresholds. Only the carry bits of each comparison need temporary qubits, and measurement clears them without further Toffoli gates. At maturity the circuit builds the payoff angle, then a reverse sweep restores the workspace.

The circuits were found in an evaluator-guided loop. Language-model agents proposed and implemented candidate builders; a separate scorer validated each gate tape and produced every count on this page.

02

How much smaller are the circuits?

A circuit can trade gates for qubits, so we score each one by the product of its Toffoli count and peak logical qubits. The dashed curve marks every point with the same product as the reference. Anything below and to the left of it is cheaper on that measure.

Against converting every return to a price

Our conservative reconstructions of the price-space arithmetic of Chakrabarti et al., under the same angle interface, have 1,250 to 2,180 times the product for the autocallable and 966 to 1,100 times for the TARF. Most of that gap comes from removing coherent exponentials, which the log-return reference already does.

A calibration outside finance

On the HHL rotation-angle task of Gu et al., the same process found a signed-Booth circuit with 987 Toffolis and 113 qubits, a product 28% below the best published circuit, with an all-input logical proof.

03

Two contracts that remember their past

Both benchmarks come from Chakrabarti et al. Their payoff depends on what has already happened along the path, so the circuit must carry contract history as well as the market state.

3 assets · 20 dates · 300 input qubits

Worst-of autocallable

On each call date, if every asset is at least 10% up, the contract pays early and ends. Otherwise, if any asset ever fell 30%, a loss linked to the worst performer can apply at maturity.

Kept between dates Three exact index sums, a knock-in counter and call records. Hardened versions keep signed distances to the barrier, so a sign bit is the barrier test for every asset at every date.

1 factor · 26 dates · 130 input qubits

Target accrual redemption forward

Each date pays a signed amount that depends on the price, unless a knock-out barrier ends the contract. Once the accrued total reaches the target, the last payment is capped and the contract stops.

Kept between dates A seven-bit windowed index, the shifted accrual and a small discount correction. A lookup supplies each date’s payment; one product at the cap date fixes the final amount exactly.

The cheapest circuit is not the safest

A circuit can compute its integer rules exactly and still disagree with the real contract when a path lands right on a barrier or the accrual cap. We stress each design with inputs chosen to sit on those edges. Narrowing registers saves gates but makes those edges harder to get right.

Selected boundary cases are deliberately hard and are not drawn from the loading law. On one million ordinary paths, every circuit here has at most four angle violations, within the 10−5 tolerance budget.

Exact where it can be proved

For the factor-sum and hardened autocallable circuits, proofs show the integer specification holds for every input, accumulator and measurement record, including phase cancellation, path preservation and workspace restoration. Lattice calculations put their probability of exceeding the angle tolerance at 6.248 × 10−8 and 3.56 × 10−8.

Tuned to the parameters

Change the volatilities and correlation and a directly transferred factor-sum circuit has 5.58 times the reference product. Refitting the integer forms to the new setting recovers a 4.29× saving. The savings come from the structure of each setting, not from a universal circuit.

05

What the numbers cover

Payoff arithmetic only

The counts cover the reversible arithmetic between loading a path and rotating the payoff into an amplitude. They exclude state preparation, the payoff rotation and amplitude estimation itself, so they are not a full cost for pricing a trade. Within that boundary, every comparison uses the same tasks, angle format and target tolerance, and the reference circuits already avoid coherent exponentials.
