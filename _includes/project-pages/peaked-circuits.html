Finding the peak on Helios-1

BlueQubit’s P11 and P12 circuits each favour a hidden 98-bit output. The organiser can check that output directly, which makes these circuits useful for testing whether a device can recover a known answer through noise.

I ran both circuits on Quantinuum Helios-1. All 50 P11 shots were different, so counting repeated strings gave a 50-way tie. In P12, the answer appeared three times in 200 shots.

I compared shots by how many bits differed. The medoid, the observed string closest to the rest in total, recovered both answers. So did voting bit by bit within the largest cluster of similar shots.

02

What the circuit files give away

The qsim-lab solver looks for rotations that reappear later as their inverses. Those matches reveal the circuit’s mirrored blocks and how the qubit wires move between them. Approximating the middle block with a wire permutation leaves about one eighth of the original two-qubit units to simulate.

The solver contracts this smaller circuit to estimate each output bit’s most likely value, then scores the resulting string by its amplitude. It tests a few gates at the boundary of the reduced circuit and keeps a move when it improves the candidate’s probability.

A boundary move affects only the output bits that depend on the moved gate. The solver can reuse the other bits’ probabilities. On a MacBook M3 Pro, caching brought the same search down to about a third of its original runtime.

Boundary search and benchmark notes +

Dylan Neve’s qsim-lab submission introduced the circuit reduction and boundary repair. I reproduced the search and cached the output marginals by their backward causal cones. I also reused contraction paths and kept the result of the accepted trial. qsim-lab adopted the cached evaluator in v2.

The cache reused 78.1% of requested marginals on P11 and 82.8% on P12. A fresh contraction confirmed each final marginal. The search times exclude imports, numerical certification, and those extra cache checks. Numerical certification established the unique mode of each reduced core to a tolerance of 10⁻¹⁰.
