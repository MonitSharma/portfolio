Classical follow-up

The same answers from the circuit files

Dylan Neve’s qsim-lab developed the original classical solver. I reproduced both results and improved the boundary search with cached exact marginals, reusable contraction paths, and stable candidate ordering. qsim-lab adopted my cached evaluator in its v2 solver.

One run per setting with the same deterministic search. Solve times exclude imports, certification, and cache validation. The tracker issues separately report qsim-lab’s original VM runs of 89 s for P11 and 182 s for P12.

How the solver works

  1. Match surviving inverse rotations between CZ gates to locate nested mirror blocks and infer how they permute the wires. Approximate the obfuscated middle by that permutation, leaving a shallow core.
  2. Contract the reduced core with quimb and cotengra to compute each output bit’s marginal. Combine the more likely bits into a candidate, then contract its amplitude to score it.
  3. Try moving frontier gates into the core and choose the best qualifying score improvement in a stable order. When a trial leaves a bit’s backward causal cone unchanged, reuse its marginal. When the tensor layout matches, reuse the contraction path.

The original team added boundary repair after its first P12 attempt missed four bits. The solver takes the QASM as input. The verification script checks agreement with the published answers after solving. My reproduction certifies each candidate as the unique mode of its reduced core to numerical tolerance. The middle-block approximation limits that certificate and the reported probabilities to the core.

The tracker verified the classical P11 submission. A reviewer reproduced both solver versions for P12 in its open issue.
