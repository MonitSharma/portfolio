Recovering the hidden answers of two 98-qubit circuits

Peaked Circuit Recovery

I recovered the P11 and P12 answers from noisy Quantinuum Helios-1 samples, and both were accepted. A later classical solver recovered the same answers from the circuit files.

P11 and P12 are circuits built to favour a hidden 98-bit output. The challenge is to recover that string from the published circuit. Because the organiser knows the answer, submissions can be checked directly.

The quantum runs

I ran both circuits on Quantinuum Helios-1. P11 returned 50 different strings. P12 returned 198 different strings from 200 shots. The accepted answer appeared once in P11 and three times in P12.

Frequency alone could not pick out P11’s answer, since every string occurred once. I used the observed medoid, the sample with the smallest total Hamming distance to the other shots, and a consensus vote within the largest cluster of nearby strings. Both recovered all 98 bits. Voting across the entire dataset missed two bits on P11 and thirteen on P12.

The classical solve

Dylan Neve’s qsim-lab solver looks for rotations that reappear later as their inverses. These pairs reveal the circuit’s mirrored blocks and how the wires move through them. Replacing most of the middle with a wire map leaves a much smaller model to contract. The final cores contain 284 two-qubit units for P11 and 279 for P12.

{% assign reduction = project.article_figures.reduction %}

The solver contracts the core to calculate the probability of zero or one at each output bit. It forms a candidate from those marginals, scores the candidate’s amplitude, and tests whether adding a frontier gate improves the result. A gate move often leaves most bits’ backward causal cones unchanged, so their exact marginals can be reused.

I reproduced the solver and added marginal and contraction-path caching, stable candidate ordering, and reuse of the accepted trial. On my MacBook M3 Pro, the same search ran about three times faster with caching. qsim-lab adopted the cached evaluator in its v2 solver.

Boundary repair, timing records and additional figures

The original team added boundary repair after an early P12 attempt missed four bits. The recorded reproduction chooses the best trial that improves the current candidate probability by at least 30%. It selects gate 59 for P11 and gates 60 and 64 for P12. Published answers are compared after solving.

The cache reused 78.1% of requested marginals on P11 and 82.8% on P12. Every final cached marginal was checked against a fresh contraction. Numerical certification then checked one competing amplitude for P11 and 31 for P12, establishing each reduced core’s unique mode to a tolerance of 10⁻¹⁰.
