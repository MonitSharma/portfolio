01

The arithmetic inside Shor

Shor finds factors by looking for a repeating pattern in powers taken modulo the RSA number. Computing those powers takes most of the work. In Gidney’s low-qubit circuit, lookups and a residue-cleanup stage account for about 84% of the Toffoli gates.

The same input bits select a row in several tables, one for each small prime. We pack their answers into a wider row, so a single lookup returns them together. In a controlled comparison across three primes, sharing alone reduced the estimated Toffoli count by 30.7%.

We also retain intermediate values to avoid recomputing them during cleanup, and let residues grow before reducing them once at the end. The paper proves that, with sufficiently wide registers, the output and relative quantum phases are preserved.

The updated paper adds a set of further arithmetic refinements: narrower shared lookup words, nine primes per lookup, measurement-based cleanup of partial sums, non-restoring division, half-period discrete logarithms with a half-modulus quotient, banked table lookups and a shorter final Chinese-remainder step. Each is checked by exhaustive and randomized tests. Those that would also help the reference circuit are given to it, so they make the comparison harder for us, not easier.

A reader’s suggestion led to one more simplification. Several steps of the residue multiplication act on a register that still holds zero, so their result is fixed by the lookup address alone. Replacing each with a single table write lowers every circuit by 5–14%, the reference included. We apply it to both sides of every comparison, so the savings above are measured against a reference that is cheaper too.

Uncertainty. For 27 circuits we repeated the RSA-2048 projection with 8 independent replicates. The 95% confidence half-width of the mean Toffoli count is at most 0.14%, far smaller than the reductions. These intervals cover sampling only, not operation prices, hardware timing or layouts.

05

Fewer gates, but how much faster?

A quantum computer also has to move data, correct errors, and supply resources for gates. Sharing a lookup reduces the gate count but makes its output wider. How much time that saves depends on what the machine can handle efficiently.

Surface code · up to 2.9× faster

The nine-prime circuit (row C) takes 1.22 days against 3.10 days for the tuned reference; a fast-phase variant reaches 1.06 days. In a simple layout model, the six-prime circuit needs 1.32 million physical qubits for 1.36 days (reference: 980,000 for 3.10 days).

Neutral atoms · it depends on the processor

On the balanced processor, the two-prime circuit takes 19.9 years against 25.4 within the published 1,480-qubit memory. On the space-efficient processor the wide-word circuits are slower than the reference; only the narrow ones win.

What the work adds to Shor

It gives hardware designers a cheaper implementation of Shor’s modular arithmetic that keeps the reference interface, so it composes with architecture-level advances such as Pinnacle and Cain et al. It also shows that there is no single best circuit: the right amount of sharing depends on the machine. The next step is to compile these circuits for a specific architecture and check whether the projected savings hold once data movement and scheduling are included. All numbers come from version 2.5 of the open benchmark, which lets others check and improve them.
