01

Where the circuit saves work

The challenge measures one elliptic-curve point addition. Toffolis can be expensive in a fault-tolerant circuit, so it pays to look for places where the same Boolean product is computed twice.

Hold an intermediate result

The final pass of my low-gate circuit found thirteen places where an idle wire could keep a product until it was needed again. The pass checks that the wire starts at zero and that the rest of the circuit leaves it alone while it holds the product. If the later target also starts at zero, CNOTs can transfer the value and restore the idle wire to zero.

Simplify classical controls

My separate low-product circuit simplifies the classical conditions that decide which quantum gates execute. The optimizer checks each Boolean identity over all assignments to the inputs it tracks. That saves gates while keeping the circuit at the same width.

03

What the benchmark checks

Both submissions passed the 9,024-shot benchmark in classical simulation. It checks the output point, phase, and workspace cleanup. The inherited arithmetic is approximate, so I also compared the circuits with their references on fresh inputs.

Fresh-input checks +

The low-gate circuit and its reference each ran on the same 2,097,152 fresh inputs. Both produced 1,151 wrong-point shots and 629 phase-failing shots, with no dirty-workspace shots. The low-product circuit had a separate paired test with 16,777,216 shots per source.

The benchmark derives its inputs from the circuit. I froze the arithmetic before screening identity tails and selected the first passing tail in each documented search.
